How we score

The full method behind your Floor Score. No black boxes — check our math.

1. What you tell us

The score is built entirely from five inputs you provide — we never see your models, data, or cloud accounts:

  • Monthly AI cloud spend (USD)
  • Primary model size bucket — under 1B, 1–7B, 7–70B, or over 70B parameters
  • Task types — classification, summarization, text generation, embeddings, code completion
  • Training / fine-tune runs per month
  • Cloud provider

2. Model size → a representative number

Each size bucket maps to a representative parameter count used in the calculation:

Bucket you pickRepresentative size
Under 1B0.5B
1B – 7B4B
7B – 70B35B
Over 70B100B

3. Right-sized for your tasks

Each task type has a published, task-optimal model size — the smallest model class that consistently matches large-model quality on that task. We average the optimal sizes of the tasks you selected:

TaskTask-optimal size
Classification1B
Summarization7B
Text generation13B
Embeddings0.1B
Code completion7B

4. The oversize ratio and your grade

oversize ratio = representative model size ÷ average task-optimal size

The ratio is the core of the score. It answers one question: how much bigger is your model than the work actually needs? The letter grade follows directly:

5. "Spend at risk" — what the dollar figure means

We compute a minimal baseline — what your tasks would cost on right-sized models — and subtract it from your stated spend:

spend at risk = monthly spend − (avg optimal size × $0.0004 × training runs)

Read this number as exposure, not as a savings prediction. It is the share of your spend running on oversized capacity — an upper bound on what right-sizing could touch. We do not claim you will save this amount. Actual savings depend on your quality requirements, latency constraints, and how much of the work can genuinely move to smaller models. That is why we call it spend at risk, not waste.

Worked example

Inputs: $10,000/month spend · 7–70B bucket (→ 35B) · summarization + generation · 10 training runs · AWS

Task-optimal average: (7B + 13B) ÷ 2 = 10B

Oversize ratio: 35 ÷ 10 = 3.5× → Grade C

Baseline: 10 × $0.0004 × 10 = $0.04

Monthly spend at risk: $10,000 − $0.04 ≈ $10,000 — nearly all of the spend is exposed to the oversizing, which is what a 3.5× ratio means. The insight is the ratio; the dollar figure shows how much spend sits under it.

6. Carbon estimate

GPU hours are estimated as model size × training runs × 0.1, converted at 0.4 kWh per GPU hour, and multiplied by the US average grid intensity of 0.429 kg CO₂/kWh (CodeCarbon open methodology). This is a directional estimate, not formal carbon accounting.

7. Honest limits

  • Self-reported inputs. The score reflects what you tell us. It is an architectural judgment, not a measurement of your bill.
  • Task type only. "Right-sized" is judged against task type. It does not account for quality requirements, latency constraints, context length, or safety margins that may legitimately demand a larger model.
  • No inference inputs yet. Most AI spend is inference; the current form weights training runs. Inference-volume inputs are on the roadmap and will sharpen the score significantly.
  • Estimates, not professional advice. Use the score to start the conversation inside your team, not as the basis for production decisions.

Scoring is free and anonymous. You only share an email if you want a report sent to you — see our data policy. No signup is required, and no sales call is needed to get your score.
